The Game

Original Fortnite overview. For current information, see Fortnite News.

Fortnite

A sandbox survival game:

Save the World — Story-based PvE mode played with up to 4 players.

Creative — User-submitted custom maps and game modes accessed through public codes.

Battle Royal — An online multiplayer terrain-based shooter with two modes, Building and Zero Build.

Game Rating — 13+ Violence

A cartoon style, with minimal violence, no blood or gore.

Competing for victory using melee weapons and firearms to complete quests and eliminate opponents.

While there isn't any profanity in the game dialogue, the game's online nature could expose younger players to iffy language from random strangers.

Game

Combining a competitive shooter with an animated theme.

Played in Duos, Trios, and Teams. Fast Paced

Game length—15–20 min

The Child

Game Experience

Competing in solo, duo, trio and squads provides unique experiences for problem-solving and team building strategies.

Fortnite can be played with random players or a cultivated list of friends and family.

The game experience can vary depending on skill and game mode. In Battle Royal mode games will vary from 10–20 min on average.

Community Experience

Across all game modes, there are many different activities to complete.

Fortnite is very similar to other gaming experiences. The cooperative experience helps children and youth focus on and develop team-building and problem-solving skills to overcome the different challenges presented.

Other attributes players can focus on are resilience and gratitude. Concentrating on learning and growing in trying to develop their skills. Gratitude would be another perspective to focus on.

The Parent

Parent Engagement

As parents we need to evolve our understanding of technology and how it impacts our children.

Reflective conversations with our children can help maintain the connection between the family values and their digital participation.

The pathway is designed to help parents engage reflectively with their child as core skills are developed.

Like a ladder we start at the bottom and use each rung as an opportunity to ask questions that engages our children in their unique experiences and games.

Family Impact

Games today are much different.

Games today do not have a pause option. However, they are much shorter, providing for breaks.

  • The popular games are more adventurous and multifaceted.
  • Many games now support multi-generational participation.
  • Many games have educational benefits that can directly impact both digital and in-person decisions.

The Pathway

Gratitude

Is noticing and appreciating both the good and challenging things that happen in life. Gratitude leads to positive and joyful outcomes.

Resilience

The parent-child relationship can be critical in helping overcome difficult experiences. In gaming the term ‘GG Next’ mean to move on to the next game.

Problem Solving

Facing tricky situations or puzzles we can ask our parents or team mates for assistance in learning how to overcome.

Team-Building

Teaches cooperation and communication through various activities and challenges helps build trust and self-confidence. The parent child relationship is a perfect place to start.
